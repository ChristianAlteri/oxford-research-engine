# Storage Strategy — Research TL;DR
*One-pager for internal discussion. Summarises three ORE research sessions: ideas explored, what survived, what didn't.*

---

## The core question across all sessions
**After a year of online aggregation with low traction, what is the right strategic direction for Storage — and what can we actually execute without external capital?**

---

## Session 1 — "Is the pivot to experiential + memberships right?"

**Starting thesis:** Pivot from online sales vertical → community-led experiential retail in London + tiered memberships (B2C £180–250/month, B2B) leveraging Stella's network.

### Ideas explored

| Idea | What it gets right | What kills it |
|---|---|---|
| Premium membership (£180–250/mo) | Targets Storage's real asset: the community | "Fashion intelligence" isn't tangible enough to justify recurring charge. No capital for infrastructure. |
| Experiential retail in London | Community shows up for in-person experiences | £200–900/sq ft annually, 3–5 year payback. Unaffordable without investment. |
| B2B infrastructure (Archive / Faume model) | B2B recurring revenue is attractive | Archive and Faume serve 50+ enterprise brands with dedicated SaaS teams. Storage is a single curator network — wrong capabilities. |
| Pure authentication play (RealReal model) | Growing authenticated luxury resale market | Needs multi-consigner scale and authentication infrastructure at volume. Not Storage's position. |
| Vivrelle-style hybrid (curation + physical access) | Luxury access memberships are growing at 11.7% CAGR | Churn risk, high CAC, and still requires proving ongoing tangible value monthly. |

### One-line summary
> *The community is the asset, not the technology — but every proposed monetisation path either costs too much or can't justify the price.*

## Session 2 — "Push back: events, cash buyouts, brand deadstock"

**Founders pushed back on Session 1.** Three new options were put in front of ORE: (A) exclusive event access, (B) sellers selling stock to stores for cash, (C) circular brand infrastructure (e.g. JW Anderson deadstock partnership).

### Ideas explored

| Idea | What it gets right | What kills it |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Exclusive event access (£45–65 tickets)** | Archive Markets already work at £8. Proven demand. Episodic events match how the community actually engages. | Jumping from £8 to £45–65 for the same product isn't a strategy — it's just a bigger price tag. Needs a fundamentally different product to justify it. |
| **B. Sellers-to-stores cash buyout** | Clean cash transaction, creates supply certainty, no consignment complexity | Requires working capital (est. £22.5–60K/quarter) to buy inventory. Not executable without investment. |
| **C. Brand deadstock partnership (e.g. JW Anderson)** | Genuinely differentiated. Brands need a curated channel for dead stock. Editorial credibility is the moat. | Session modelled commission + monthly fees that were economically impossible for early-stage brands to justify. Model was wrong, not the idea. |
| Premium seller tiers at existing markets | Charges sellers more, not buyers. Doesn't require changing the buyer experience. | Not explored in depth — surfaced as a direction but dropped. |
| 90-day event test at £5–8K upfront | Capital-light, fast signal | Session converged here but didn't interrogate the £8 → £45 jump critically enough. |

### One-line summary
> *The right instinct is events + brand partnerships — but the session priced itself into strategies Storage can't fund and missed the technology stack entirely.*

---

## Session 3 — "The tech stack is the missing piece"

**New context injected:** The two prior sessions had no knowledge of MarketMate or the POS. Session 3 forced ORE to engage with the full technology stack and stress-test four new hypotheses across 4 rounds of increasingly brutal debate.

### Ideas explored

| Idea | What it gets right | What kills it |
|---|---|---|
| **MarketMate as B2B SaaS for market organisers** | Real feature gap vs Eventbrite + spreadsheets. Storage's markets are live proof. | Eventeny already exists at £45–180/month with 30,000+ events. Not a clear gap. |
| **Managed sample sales for brands (JW Anderson-style deadstock)** | Brands do struggle with sample sale logistics. Fashion credibility is a genuine differentiator. | Showcase.co already dominates this market with a Regent Street flagship, global ops, and clients including Dior, Gucci, Prada, Valentino. Unwinnable. |
| **Managed service for emerging designer deadstock** | Real pain point — young UK designers can't afford traditional sample sale infrastructure. | Designers have 20–50 pieces per collection, not enough volume. Price point mismatch with Archive Markets' £8 buyer base. Economically unviable. |
| **MarketMate licensing to established vintage fairs** | £7bn UK secondhand market, 11.12% CAGR. Existing operators need better tools. | Frock Me!, Judy's Vintage Fair, Clerkenwell all already have booking systems, payment processing, and dashboards. Not actually behind. |
| **Premium seller tiers at Archive Markets** | Charges sellers not buyers. Uses existing MarketMate infrastructure. No buyer price shock. | Projected revenue: ~£12K/year. Not enough to run a business. Analytics pitch undercut by Vinted/Depop which offer free analytics at scale. |
| **MarketMate operational cost savings** | MarketMate does reduce admin overhead on Storage's own events. | Venue hire is a fixed cost no software eliminates. Projected annual savings ~£3K. If that's a meaningful number, the underlying business has a margin problem. |

### The hard finding from Session 3
The Skeptic delivered a fatal blow in Round 4 that the session stopped on:

> *"If Storage views £3,000 annual savings as strategically significant, the core business is operating on unsustainably thin margins. Archive Markets alone cannot support a sustainable business. Either 10x event revenue or pivot to MarketMate/POS as the primary revenue engine — even if that means competing in established markets."*

### One-line summary
> *Every external opportunity faces an entrenched competitor. The internal business runs on thin margins. The honest conclusion: Archive Markets is a proof-of-concept, not a business — and MarketMate may be the actual product, just not yet sold externally.*

## What has survived across all three sessions

- The community (Stella's network, sellers, influencers) is the genuine asset
- In-person events are the proven format. Online has consistently failed to generate urgency or stickiness
- The £8 Archive Market price point has trained buyers — a price jump to £45–65 requires a fundamentally different product, not just a bigger number
- MarketMate is a real, differentiated product — the question is who pays for it and at what price
- The POS + Inventory system has one confirmed happy customer. That's a signal worth following
- Archive Markets is the best proof-of-concept Storage has. It's not yet a revenue engine

## What has been killed across all three sessions

- ~~Luxury membership at £180–250/month~~ — no tangible product, no capital to build infrastructure
- ~~London retail lease / flagship experiential store~~ — unaffordable
- ~~Competing with Archive / Faume as enterprise SaaS~~ — wrong capabilities, wrong scale
- ~~The RealReal authentication play~~ — wrong inventory depth
- ~~Cash inventory buyout model~~ — requires working capital Storage doesn't have
- ~~Managed sample sales for luxury brands~~ — Showcase.co already owns this market
- ~~Emerging designer deadstock managed service~~ — price point mismatch, too little volume
- ~~MarketMate licensed to established vintage fairs~~ — incumbents already have their own systems
- ~~Premium seller tiers as a business~~ — projected revenue (~£12K/year) too small to matter
- ~~MarketMate cost savings as a strategy~~ — £3K/year savings signals a thin-margin problem, not a solution

---

## The question the sessions haven't answered yet

**If Archive Markets is the proof-of-concept and MarketMate is the real product — who is the right buyer for MarketMate that isn't already served by Eventeny, and what would they pay?**

This is the open question. The Skeptic killed every external market attempt on competitive grounds, but the sessions may have been too quick to rule out niches where Storage's *fashion-specific* credibility (not just the software features) is the differentiator. That angle wasn't fully explored.

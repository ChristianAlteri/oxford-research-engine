The technology Stack Storage actually has (THIS IS NEW CONTEXT — THE PRIOR SESSIONS DID NOT KNOW THIS)

1. MarketMate (marketmate.live)

A full-stack event management SaaS platform built and operated by the Storage team. Features: customisable vendor application forms, Stripe-native invoicing and Instant Checkout, ticket sales with QR code check-in, add-on product sales, stall maps, attendee management, branded transactional emails (via Resend), Meta Pixel + Conversions API (CAPI) built in, per-ticket and per-product analytics. It is currently live and used to run markets. It is a one-time install managed by the team.

Key insight: MarketMate is the operational backbone for running curated markets and events. Other market and event organisers do not have this tool. Eventbrite, Typeform, Square, Google Forms, Shopify — none of them combine vendor applications + invoicing + ticket sales + stall management + branded emails + Meta attribution in one product.

2. POS + Inventory Management

Storage has built and deployed a Point of Sale (POS) system with integrated inventory management. One London vintage store is already using it and loves it — specifically the inventory management component. The founders believe every store on the Storage platform will eventually use this POS. The constraint: vintage stores are comfortable being small. They have no ambition to scale. They are not a growth market for SaaS. The real opportunity may be with brands who DO want to scale their circular/resale operations.

The real strategic question the prior sessions missed

Storage has two assets that prior sessions completely ignored:

  1. MarketMate — an operational platform that event and market organisers need and don't have. This is a SaaS or service revenue opportunity that has nothing to do with vintage fashion per se.
  2. POS + Inventory — a tool that's already proven with one store. Vintage stores won't scale, but brands running circular/resale operations (deadstock, returns, rental, second-hand) might.

The question is: can Storage use its tech stack as the wedge — not just to serve its own community, but to sell to OTHER curated market organisers, OTHER brand circular programmes, or OTHER vintage communities — and use StoragePlatform as the showcase proof-of-concept?

The pricing problem (critical — must address)

Archive Markets currently charge £8 entry. The prior session recommended £45–65. The founders reject this because:

  • The audience has been trained to expect £8. A 5–8x price increase requires a completely different product, not the same market with a bigger price tag.
  • A separate premium tier (members-only pre-opening, exclusive sellers, curated edit) could coexist at higher price points — but requires a genuine value difference that justifies it.
  • Or: stop trying to charge buyers more and instead charge SELLERS more — or charge OTHER market organisers to use the platform.

New hypotheses to stress-test (the prior sessions never considered these)

Hypothesis 1 — MarketMate as the business, Storage as the proof

MarketMate is already a standalone SaaS product. Storage's own markets are its live proof of concept. Could MarketMate be sold/licensed to other curated market organisers in London (vintage, food, art, craft) as a B2B SaaS subscription? These organisers currently use Eventbrite + spreadsheets + bank transfers. What is the total addressable market of curated market organisers in London? What would they pay per month or per event for MarketMate? This generates B2B SaaS revenue completely independent of Storage's fashion community — while Storage's community events remain the showcase.

Hypothesis 2 — POS + Inventory as a brand circular infrastructure tool

Brands running deadstock sales, circular programmes, or pop-up resale (e.g. JW Anderson, smaller independent labels) need a POS and inventory system that vintage store owners find too complex or unnecessary. Could Storage's POS become the operational layer for a brand's circular event or pop-up — offered as a managed service (Storage runs the event tech) in exchange for a revenue share or fixed fee? This is not Archive (enterprise SaaS) — it's a hands-on managed service for 1–3 brand partners to start.

Hypothesis 3 — Stack the revenue on existing Archive Markets without raising ticket prices

Instead of charging buyers more, charge SELLERS more — or introduce seller tiers. Currently sellers pay a stall fee. What if Storage introduced: (a) a premium seller tier with better placement, more marketing, and early access that costs 2–3x the standard stall fee; (b) a "featured seller" package that includes online pre-event listing, social amplification, and post-event analytics for an additional fee; (c) a "Storage Verified" badge that sellers pay for annually, which acts as a light certification/curation signal. None of these require charging buyers more. All of them leverage MarketMate's existing infrastructure.

Hypothesis 4 — Sell MarketMate's event management to vintage and curated market scenes outside London

Storage has operational credibility from running their own markets. Could they white-label or resell MarketMate to market organisers in other UK cities or abroad — with Storage taking a percentage of each event's vendor revenue as a platform fee? This is a distribution/licensing play, not a capital play.
